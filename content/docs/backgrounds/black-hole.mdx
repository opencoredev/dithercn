## Usage

```tsx
import { BlackHole } from "@/components/dithercn/black-hole";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <BlackHole className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

A pure black shadow sits at the centre, ringed by a thin, solid photon ring. Around it, a hot accretion disk is seen at a slant: `tilt` sets how edge-on it is. The near half of the disk crosses in front of the shadow as a flat band, and the lens bends the far half up into an arc over the top of the shadow and, as a thinner image, under the bottom, so the disk seems to wrap round the hole. A thin dark gap runs between the photon ring and the disk all the way round. A sparse field of stars sits behind, and the ones near the hole bend into short arcs.

Lanes of gas orbit the hole, the inner ones several times faster than the rim, each carrying brighter and darker streaks, so the disk visibly turns. The side turning towards you is brighter, as a real disk's is: the right with `direction="clockwise"`, the left with `"counterclockwise"`.

By default the palette maps brightness to colour, from paper to the last colour, so the brightest gas and the photon ring take the last colour. A palette such as `["--dither-paper", "orange", "#ffe9d6"]` gives the familiar white-hot disk. Set `colorField` to colour the dots by temperature instead: the last colour on the photon ring and the hot inner edge, fading through the palette to the first ink colour at the cool rim, with a palette colour for each star.

The lens is faked, not traced: each cell works out which part of the disk it shows in polar coordinates round the centre, so it costs about as much as the [Galaxy](/docs/backgrounds/galaxy).

## Examples

### Warm close-up

A larger `size` crops the disk at the edges of the box, and a slight `angle` cants it. `colorField` with explicit colours, from a warm black through red and amber to white, keeps the sky dark in both themes. The 8×8 matrix gives a finer texture.

### Beside a headline

The hole sits on the right, canted slightly, and turns clockwise, so its dim side faces the text on the left and the bright side is on the far edge. A soft tint of the ink between paper and ink fills in the outer disk, so it reads as a glow rather than scattered dots. Below 560px wide, the example moves the `center` to the top middle and the text to the bottom, so the disk stays in frame and clear of the copy.

## Notes

- The black hole is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a still SVG of the same scene in smooth gradients: the shadow, the photon ring, the near half of the disk in front, the lensed arcs over and under the shadow, the brighter side and a scatter of stars, in the same colours.
- On a light theme the paper is light and the disk is drawn in ink, so the shadow reads as a pale disc ringed in ink, like a printed negative, and the hottest gas is the darkest. Pass a dark first palette colour, as the close-up does, for a black sky on any theme.
