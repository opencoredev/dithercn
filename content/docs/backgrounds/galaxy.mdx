## Usage

```tsx
import { Galaxy } from "@/components/dithercn/galaxy";

export function Hero() {
  return (
    <section className="relative isolate min-h-[480px]">
      <Galaxy className="absolute inset-0 -z-10" />
      <h1>Your headline</h1>
    </section>
  );
}
```

The galaxy is a flat disc seen at an angle: `tilt` leans it away from you and `angle` turns its long axis on the screen. A round bulge glows at the centre, and logarithmic arms wind out of it, with knots of bright stars along them and dark dust lanes on their inner edges. A faint halo surrounds the disc, and sparse stars sit fixed behind it.

The arms turn slowly as one piece, the way a real spiral pattern does, while the stars and dust stream through them, faster near the core than at the rim. That shear is reset out of sight every few seconds, so the spiral keeps its shape however long the page stays open.

By default the palette maps brightness to colour, from paper to the last colour. Set `colorField` to colour the dots by place instead: the last colour at the core, the first ink colour on the arms, and a random palette colour for each background star.

## Examples

### Nearly edge-on

`tilt={76}` lays the disc almost flat, so the dust on the near side cuts a dark band across the bulge. Three arms, a tighter `twist` and `colorField` with a four-colour palette give a warm core that passes through purple into cool arms.

### Beside a headline

The galaxy sits high on the right and turns counterclockwise, and the text sits in the clear space on the left, or below it on a phone. `size` is a share of the shorter side, so the disc stays in frame on narrow screens.

## Notes

- The galaxy is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. Put real text on top of it, not inside it.
- With reduced motion turned on, it draws one still frame and stops.
- It pauses when scrolled out of view or when the tab is hidden, and releases its WebGL context when far offscreen. Without WebGL it shows a tilted CSS glow with faint arms and a few stars, in the same colours.
- On a light theme the paper is light and the galaxy is drawn in ink, so the dust lanes read as pale gaps, like a printed negative. Pass a dark first palette colour for a dark sky on any theme.
