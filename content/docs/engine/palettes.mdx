A palette is the list of colours a dither can use, ordered from paper (tone 0) to ink (tone 1). By default it's two colours, `--dither-paper` and `--dither-ink`, which [`dither-tokens`](/docs/theming) points at your `--background` and `--primary`.

## More than two colours

Components that draw with `DitherShader` take a `palette` prop with two or more colours. The tone is spread across the list, and each cell is dithered between the two neighbouring colours. With four colours, a tone of 0.5 sits halfway between the second and third:

```tsx
<DitherGradient
  palette={["--background", "--chart-3", "--chart-2", "--chart-1"]}
/>
```

Each entry is a CSS variable name such as `"--chart-1"`, or any CSS colour: `"var(--chart-1)"`, `"oklch(0.72 0.15 272)"`, `"#7c8cff"`, even `"transparent"` to let the page show through the paper. The type is `DitherPalette`, a tuple of at least two `DitherColor` strings, so a one-colour palette fails to compile.

Order matters more than hue. Put colours in order of how much they stand out against the paper, or the ramp will jump back and forth.

## Any number of hues

A field returns one tone per cell, and the palette turns that tone into a colour. So with a palette of several hues, the tone decides both how dense the dots are and which colour they take. That suits ramps, such as a glow that runs from deep blue to white, but it can't draw a red line and a blue line at the same strength, or a spectrum that runs across the box.

For that, keep shape and colour apart. Draw the shape with the field, and put the colour in a layer underneath. A palette of `["--background", "transparent"]` paints the page background where the tone is 0 and lets the layer show through where it is 1, with dithered steps in between:

</ComponentPreview>

```tsx
<div className="relative isolate overflow-hidden">
  <div
    aria-hidden
    className="absolute inset-0"
    style={{
      background:
        "linear-gradient(to bottom, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet)",
    }}
  />
  <DitherShader
    field={FIELD}
    palette={["--background", "transparent"]}
    className="absolute inset-0"
  />
</div>
```

The layer can be any CSS, SVG or canvas: a `linear-gradient` for hue that changes across the box, a `conic-gradient` for a ring whose colour runs around it, or an animated `background-position` for colour that drifts. When a scene has several shapes, give each its own layer positioned over it, a little larger than the shape and its glow. One gradient under everything gives every shape the colour at its height. The dither still does the shaping, so soft edges and glows fall off in dots of whatever colour sits underneath.

You can also stack one shader per colour, each with a palette of `["transparent", colour]`, but every shader takes a WebGL context and at most eight run at once. One shader over a colour layer is cheaper.

Literal colours don't follow the theme, so check them in light mode: yellow and cyan that glow on black can wash out on white. Pick darker stops for light mode, or set the colours as CSS variables with light and dark values.

## Following the theme

Colours are resolved in the browser, in the context of the component, so a variable you override on a wrapper applies to everything inside it. Formats the GPU can't read, such as `oklch()`, are converted by painting them once into a 1×1 canvas.

When the class or style on `<html>` changes, as it does when next-themes switches between light and dark, every dither re-reads its colours and redraws. Nothing else needs to happen.

For your own canvas work, `useDitherColors` does the same and returns the colours as `[r, g, b, a]` in [0, 1]:

```tsx
const ref = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);
const colors = useDitherColors(ref); // [ink, paper], or null before mount
```

Pass a list of names as the second argument to read other variables, such as `useDitherColors(ref, ["--chart-1", "--chart-2"])`.

## Static fills

`DitherPattern` takes a `color` and an optional `background`, and `ditherMaskStyle` masks whatever background colour the element already has. Both accept CSS variables, so they follow the theme without JavaScript.

## Contrast

A dither reads only when ink and paper differ enough. For charts and anything that carries information, aim for the contrast you would want for body text, about 4.5:1. Quiet backgrounds can go lower, since nobody needs to read them. The defaults, your primary on your background, usually clear that bar in both modes.
